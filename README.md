> A stranger's laundry snaps on the line, and for a second someone else's Tuesday is waving at me.

## Why it works

The moment is as small and everyday as it gets: a stranger's laundry drying on a line. The surprise is in the phrasing itself — the laundry is not drying, it is *waving*, and what waves at me is not a hand but "someone else's Tuesday": a stranger's plainest weekday, borrowed for a second and made to greet me. The image lands in one read (snap, then wave, then stop), and it stays grounded in things you can stand under — a line, wind, a Tuesday. Nothing in it is filler: "stranger's" sets the distance the wave then closes, "snaps" carries the sound and the motion, "for a second" makes the wonder brief and therefore true, and "Tuesday" is the most ordinary day there is, which is exactly why it surprises.

## Run

```sh
node main.ts
```

Writes the sentence, exactly and with no trailing newline, to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (default `/output/entry.txt`), mode 0644. Node 22.18+ (the grader runs Node 22.23.4).
